
Dr. Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar (1891-1956) was an Indian jurist, economist, politician and social reformer who inspired the Dalit Buddhist movement and campaigned against social discrimination towards the untouchables (Dalits).

He was independent India's first Minister of Law and Justice, and the chief architect of the Constitution of India. Ambedkar was a prolific student, earning doctorates in economics from both Columbia University and the London School of Economics.

Ambedkar's legacy includes numerous memorials and depictions in popular culture. He is considered the father of the Indian Constitution and a champion of human rights. His work in economics, sociology and anthropology had a lasting impact on India's development.

Key achievements include:
- Drafting the Indian Constitution
- Establishing the Reserve Bank of India
- Fighting for women's rights and labor rights
- Promoting education among oppressed communities
- Converting to Buddhism along with 500,000 followers

His famous quote: "I measure the progress of a community by the degree of progress which women have achieved."
